You met the two phases in [0.2](/learn/m00/life-of-a-request) and [1.4](/learn/m01/autoregressive-generation). With arithmetic intensity
([3.2](/learn/m03/arithmetic-intensity)) you can now predict how long each one takes.

## Two phases, two limits

**Prefill** runs all $T$ prompt tokens through the model at once. Every weight matmul has $M = T$ rows, so its intensity is in the
hundreds or thousands: **compute-bound**. Its time is about

$$
\text{TTFT} \approx \frac{2N \cdot T + \text{attention}}{P_{\text{peak}}}
$$

**Decode** runs one new token per sequence. At batch $b$, matmul intensity is about $b$, and attention intensity is about 1–8 regardless of
batch. Unless $b$ is in the hundreds, it is **memory-bound**:

$$
\text{TPOT} \approx \frac{\text{weight bytes} + b \times \text{KV bytes per sequence}}{B_{\text{HBM}}}
$$

For Llama-3-8B on an H100, a 2,048-token prompt and 256 output tokens, the roofline gives **TTFT ≈ 30 ms** and **TPOT ≈ 4.6 ms**. The whole
answer takes about 1.2 s, and 97% of it is decode.

<Callout type="try" title="Try these">
  1. Switch the GPU from **H100** to **H200**. TTFT hardly changes (same compute), while TPOT drops by about 30% (more bandwidth).
  2. Raise **concurrent users** from 1 to 64 on Llama-3-8B. TPOT roughly doubles, from 4.6 to 10 ms, but total throughput rises from about 220 to 6,400 tokens/s.
  3. Do the same with **Llama-2-7B**. At 64 users TPOT reaches about 26 ms: without grouped-query attention, the per-user KV cache is four times larger, and reading it dominates the step.
  4. Raise the prompt from 512 to 8,192 tokens. TTFT grows from about 8 ms to 134 ms: faster than linear, because attention cost grows with $T^2$.
</Callout>

## Batching: the central trade

Decode at batch 1 wastes almost all of the GPU's arithmetic. Adding sequences to a step reuses the weights already being loaded, so
throughput rises nearly in proportion while TPOT grows slowly, until the KV cache reads take over. That gives every serving engine its core
dial:

- **Small batches:** fast tokens for each user, expensive per token.
- **Large batches:** cheap tokens, slower for each user, and more memory spent on KV cache.

The right setting depends on what users were promised: a **service-level objective** (SLO) such as "TTFT under 500 ms and TPOT under 50 ms
at the 99th percentile". Chapters [13.1](/learn/m13/serving-metrics) and [16.2](/learn/m16/slos-goodput) turn that into numbers you can measure.

## Why the phases interfere

A serving engine runs both phases for different users on the same GPU. When a new request's prefill enters a step, that step becomes
compute-bound and much longer, and **every user in the decode batch waits** for it. A 30 ms prefill inserted into a stream of 5 ms decode
steps is a visible stutter for everyone.

<Callout type="insight" title="This conflict shapes modern serving">
  Engines answer it in two ways. **Chunked prefill** splits long prompts into pieces and mixes them into decode steps, so no single step
  gets too long ([10.3](/learn/m10/chunked-prefill)). **Disaggregated serving** runs prefill and decode on separate GPUs, each configured for
  its own limit, and transfers the KV cache between them ([15.1](/learn/m15/why-disaggregate)).
</Callout>
