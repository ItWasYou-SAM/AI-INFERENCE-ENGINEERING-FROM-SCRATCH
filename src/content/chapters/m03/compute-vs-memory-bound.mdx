Module 2 showed that a GPU has two separate engines: arithmetic units that can do about 989 TFLOP/s, and a memory system that delivers about
3.35 TB/s (H100 SXM). A kernel needs both, and they work in parallel. Whichever needs more time sets the pace.

## Two clocks running at once

For a kernel that does $F$ floating-point operations and moves $Q$ bytes to and from HBM:

$$
T_{\text{compute}} = \frac{F}{P_{\text{peak}}} \qquad T_{\text{memory}} = \frac{Q}{B} \qquad T \;\gtrsim\; \max\big(T_{\text{compute}},\, T_{\text{memory}}\big)
$$

- If $T_{\text{memory}} > T_{\text{compute}}$, the kernel is **memory-bound**: the arithmetic units wait for data.
- If $T_{\text{compute}} > T_{\text{memory}}$, it is **compute-bound**: the data arrives faster than it can be used.

Two worked examples on an H100:

| Kernel | FLOPs | Bytes | $T_{\text{compute}}$ | $T_{\text{memory}}$ | Bound |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|:--|
| Vector add, $10^9$ FP32 elements | $10^9$ | 12 GB | 15 µs (FP32, 67 TFLOP/s) | **3.6 ms** | memory, by 240× |
| Matmul, $8192^3$, BF16 | $1.1 \times 10^{12}$ | 0.4 GB | **1.1 ms** | 0.12 ms | compute, by 9× |

The vector add reads two numbers and writes one (12 bytes) for each single add. No amount of extra arithmetic hardware would speed it up.
The matmul reuses each loaded number thousands of times, so the memory system has time to spare.

## The third regime: overhead

Small operations often hit neither limit. Each kernel launch costs a few microseconds of CPU and driver work, and Python adds more. If a
kernel's real work takes 2 µs but launching it takes 5 µs, the kernel is **overhead-bound**, and the GPU sits idle between launches.

<Callout type="warning" title="Decode is often overhead-bound at small sizes">
  A decode step of a small model launches hundreds of small kernels per token. At batch 1, each one finishes in microseconds, so launch
  overhead can take a large share of the step. That is why engines capture the whole step as a **CUDA Graph** and replay it with one launch
  ([8.7](/learn/m08/torch-compile-cuda-graphs)). You saw the same effect on a CPU in the [1.6 lab](/learn/m01/tiny-gpt-numpy).
</Callout>

## Why the label matters

The regime tells you which optimizations can possibly help:

| If the kernel is… | These help | These do nothing |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Memory-bound** | moving fewer bytes: quantization, fusion, better reuse, batching | faster arithmetic, tensor cores, FP8 *math* |
| **Compute-bound** | tensor cores, lower-precision math, avoiding wasted FLOPs | caching, compression of data |
| **Overhead-bound** | CUDA Graphs, fusing kernels, bigger batches, less Python | anything inside the kernel |

<Callout type="insight">
  Most LLM decode work is memory-bound, and most prefill work is compute-bound ([3.4](/learn/m03/prefill-vs-decode)). So the same model
  needs opposite optimizations in its two phases. Many optimizations that look good in a benchmark have no effect in serving because they
  target the wrong limit.
</Callout>

## Diagnosing a real kernel

Profilers report a kernel's achieved FLOP/s and achieved bandwidth, often as a **percentage of peak** (Nsight Compute calls this
"Speed of Light", [7.2](/learn/m07/nsight-systems-vs-compute)). Read them together:

- High % of memory bandwidth, low % of compute: memory-bound and already efficient. Reduce bytes.
- High % of compute, low % of bandwidth: compute-bound. Use better math (tensor cores, lower precision).
- Low on both: neither limit is reached. Look for overhead, poor occupancy ([2.5](/learn/m02/occupancy-latency-hiding)), or a bad access pattern.

<Callout type="note" title="Exercises">
  1. A kernel applies RMSNorm to 4,096 tokens × 4,096 dims in BF16: about 5 FLOPs per element, reading and writing each element once. Is it compute- or memory-bound on an H100, and by how much?
  2. A profiler reports 2.9 TB/s and 12 TFLOP/s for a kernel on an H100. Which regime is it in, and is it well optimized?
  3. A kernel takes 4 µs and is launched 600 times per decode step. With 5 µs launch overhead each, what fraction of the step is overhead if nothing overlaps?
</Callout>
